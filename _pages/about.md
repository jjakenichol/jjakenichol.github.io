I'm a Senior Member of Technical Staff in the AI Credibility department at [Sandia National Laboratories](https://www.sandia.gov/). I study model credibility: how well the evidence shows that a model approximates the real-world process behind its data. In high-consequence settings, where models need to be reliable, that evidence has to be strong enough to act on. I lead the credibility team on a trustworthy machine learning project, co-lead a group that builds cause-and-effect models to detect threats to the power grid, and study whether targeted changes can make AI weather models more or less realistic.

My background is in causal discovery, the study of how to infer cause and effect from data. I earned my PhD in Computer Science at the University of New Mexico in 2025, advised by Melanie Moses and Matthew Fricke. There I developed [**CaStLe**](https://github.com/jjakenichol/CaStLe), a method that maps cause and effect across space and time in Earth science data, and used it to trace how the 1991 eruption of Mount Pinatubo affected the atmosphere.
